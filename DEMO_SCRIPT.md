# BarrierBreak — 3 minute demo script

Open with `/?demo=true` for the guided opener, or go straight to `/`.

**0:00 — The idea (guided opener)**
"Accessibility is a network problem. Fixing one barrier may accomplish nothing if another still
blocks the journey." Click *See an example*, then toggle **A only → B only → A + B**: 0, 0, 418.

**0:30 — The planner**
Point at the map: gray is accessible today, red dashes are blocked links, diamonds are candidate
barriers. Budget is set to $400K for an illustrative program year.

**0:50 — Optimize**
Click **Find highest-impact repair plan**. Narrate the sequence: evaluating dependencies →
selecting coordinated repair sets → newly continuous routes drawing in green. Counters rise from
0 to 976 modeled connections and coverage 61% → 78%.

**1:20 — Why these repairs?**
Click the prompt under the metrics. The inspector shows the two 228th Ave SE repairs, $162K, and
the standalone-vs-joint switcher. Say: "Either repair alone completes nothing. Together they
complete 418 trips."

**1:45 — Budget behaviour**
Drag the budget slider along the impact frontier. Note that the curve steps, not slopes: an unlock
set becomes affordable and a block of trips appears at once. Switch strategy to **Marginal greedy**
and show that it spends the same money for fewer completed trips.

**2:15 — Unlock a Journey**
Go to *Unlock a Journey*, keep the first trip, click **Unlock this journey**: the blocked route
completes and the cost appears. This is the version to show a council member who wants one story.

**2:35 — Plan Comparison**
Same ~$400K, two selection methods: 512 vs 976 modeled connections. Say clearly that this is a
difference in method, not a scorecard of any real city program.

**2:50 — Close on Method**
"Everything here is illustrative demo data. The method is the product; the data is what a city
would supply." Point at the *Method & data* tab and the demo-data badge in the header.
